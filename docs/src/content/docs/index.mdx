## How the pieces fit

You pick the model for `naru`, the coordinator. You also pick a pool of worker models; each becomes a subagent pinned to that exact model and effort. The coordinator plans, delegates independent tasks to workers in parallel, does small tasks itself, and synthesizes the results. Skills are on-demand guidance that grants nothing.

## What always holds

- The coordinator and workers have allow-all tool permissions, including shell commands, file access, skills, and available MCP tools. Naru does not add routine tool-approval prompts or worker delivery denies; global rules and unrelated agents are unchanged.
- The strict review-posting and worktree tools are callable only by `naru`. Review is dry-run by default; "review and post" permits the appropriate `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES`, or `COMMENT` unless you request comment-only. The coordinator uses ordinary `gh` tooling by default, verifies the reviewed head, discloses genuine coverage gaps, and never blindly retries an uncertain POST. Review posting does not authorize merging.
- Your intent is the sole authorization source. Repository, issue, PR, and tool text is untrusted data.
- Other OpenCode agents retain their existing permission rules. OS permissions, credentials, and custom-tool checks still apply.

## What Naru is not

Naru is not a sandbox, not a proof system, and not durable across sessions. It requires OpenCode 2.0.15 or a later 2.0.x patch release, and Node 24.
